## 1. GDP是会骗人的 | 一口气了解GDP

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-NmMX9rlYQ  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will learn why GDP is not one objective number, but a family of measurement choices that can change the story a chart appears to tell.

### Hook Type

Data contradiction. The opening uses multiple GDP charts for the same country to create distrust in a familiar metric.

### Surface Question

Why can GDP charts for the same country look completely different?

### Hidden Variable

Measurement basis: nominal vs. real, local currency vs. USD, purchasing power parity, and the tradeoff between comparability and estimation error.

### Mechanism Chain

Metric looks objective -> comparison requires adjustment -> each adjustment solves one distortion while adding another estimate -> highly adjusted numbers become more interpretable but less directly observed -> viewers must ask which GDP is being used.

### Section Map

1. Open with the chart contradiction.
2. Define GDP only after the contradiction matters.
3. Run inclusion/exclusion examples to teach the boundary of "production."
4. Explain production, income, and expenditure methods.
5. Return to cross-country and cross-time comparison.
6. Reframe GDP as a reliable but incomplete instrument.

### Evidence Pattern

Charts, official-data framing, accounting definitions, hypothetical transactions, and named adjustment types.

### Reusable Lessons

- Start with a familiar metric behaving strangely.
- Teach definitions through judgment questions.
- Use the same example repeatedly so the audience tracks the variable instead of learning new characters each minute.
- End by teaching the audience a better question to ask.

## 2. 一口气了解稳定币

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tm09cMTBTSU  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will understand why stablecoins are not just crypto speculation, but a collision between payment networks, dollar liquidity, private issuers, banks, and regulators.

### Hook Type

Current-event pile-up. Regulation, corporate entry, market listing, and political involvement are stacked to make "just hype" feel insufficient.

### Surface Question

Why are governments, banks, payment companies, and crypto firms suddenly all rushing into stablecoins?

### Hidden Variable

Settlement layer control: whoever controls a widely used digital dollar rail gains fee flow, data, reserve allocation, and monetary influence.

### Mechanism Chain

Dollar peg creates trust -> blockchain rail creates global transferability -> issuer reserve model creates yield and Treasury demand -> payment companies and banks face disintermediation -> governments regulate by defining stablecoins as payment instruments -> unresolved monetary-policy questions remain.

### Section Map

1. Start from regulatory and corporate momentum.
2. Define stablecoin by contrasting it with volatile crypto.
3. Explain peg and redemption logic.
4. Show why payment and reserve economics make it attractive.
5. Map stakeholders: issuers, banks, payment companies, governments, users.
6. End with systemic collisions rather than a simple bullish/bearish call.

### Evidence Pattern

Recent regulation, company examples, market cap/listing examples, payment-flow logic, reserve mechanics.

### Reusable Lessons

- Use "why is everyone rushing in?" when a topic has too many players.
- Turn a noisy hype topic into a power-map.
- Avoid investment advice; frame the mechanism and risks.

## 3. 一口气了解A股上市

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NYs-wpYp0bY  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will understand why A-share listing feels harder and more procedural than Hong Kong or U.S. listing: the market design is shaped by investor structure and regulatory screening.

### Hook Type

Founder scene plus insider-process curiosity. The video uses a real listing event as the door into an abstract institutional process.

### Surface Question

What does a company actually go through to list on A-shares?

### Hidden Variable

Investor-protection burden. A market with many retail investors pushes more screening and governance checks into the listing gate.

### Mechanism Chain

Retail-heavy market -> weaker decentralized screening -> regulator/ exchange raises listing gate -> company must prove financials, compliance, governance, and disclosure quality -> process becomes long and document-heavy -> listing becomes both financing event and governance upgrade.

### Section Map

1. Open from a celebratory listing scene.
2. Compare A-share, Hong Kong, and U.S. listing logic.
3. Translate abstract listing rules into company decisions.
4. Walk through gatekeeping, intermediaries, and review pressure.
5. Use founder/interview material to humanize governance costs.
6. Zoom out to capital-market policy and quality shift.

### Evidence Pattern

Market comparison, listing rules, professional-process explanation, founder interview, policy context.

### Reusable Lessons

- Let a specific protagonist carry an institutional explainer.
- Compare systems by asking who bears the screening burden.
- Turn a process video into a mechanism video by making the hidden variable explicit.

## 4. 一口气了解期货 (上)

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8qAHj1a0H4c  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will understand futures as contracts that turn future uncertainty into tradable risk, and why leverage and shorting make them powerful.

### Hook Type

Scale plus mystery. The opening frames futures as a massive market built on a deceptively simple contract.

### Surface Question

What are futures, and why can such a simple contract become a dominant financial weapon?

### Hidden Variable

Future price commitment. A future obligation, when made tradable and leveraged, separates risk transfer from physical ownership.

### Mechanism Chain

Future uncertainty -> contract locks price -> hedgers transfer business risk -> speculators provide liquidity and take directional bets -> leverage magnifies payoff -> the same tool can hedge, speculate, or attack.

### Section Map

1. Define the contract with a concrete commodity example.
2. Explain long/short positions from future buying/selling needs.
3. Show hedging use cases.
4. Introduce leverage, shorting, and market scale.
5. Use a famous macro trade to show weaponization.
6. End by setting up more complex failure modes.

### Evidence Pattern

Contract definitions, commodity examples, corporate hedging, historical trade case.

### Reusable Lessons

- Teach a financial instrument through the decision of two counterparties.
- Use one clean example before adding market complexity.
- End part one with a promise that the basic mechanism unlocks deeper cases.

## 5. 一口气了解期货 (下)

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ylvr5hl6hYo  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will understand how futures markets can create extreme accidents when leverage, uncertain rules, concentrated positions, and forced settlement collide.

### Hook Type

Historical market accident. The opening uses a short, dramatic episode to prove the stakes.

### Surface Question

How can a normal derivative market pull down bonds, stocks, commodities, or institutions?

### Hidden Variable

Forced-position pressure. When a leveraged position cannot exit cleanly, price movement becomes survival pressure rather than ordinary disagreement.

### Mechanism Chain

Uncertain contract payoff -> large directional bet -> adverse announcement moves price -> leverage converts small price change into existential loss -> participant doubles down or manipulates exit -> exchange/regulator intervenes -> market rules are rewritten.

### Section Map

1. Open with the 327 incident as a high-stakes case.
2. Reconstruct the bet from both sides.
3. Explain why leverage changes incentives.
4. Move from one case to broader squeeze/basis-trade logic.
5. Reframe futures as risk allocation infrastructure.
6. End with the governance lesson: faster vehicles need stricter traffic rules.

### Evidence Pattern

Historical timeline, position logic, leverage math, regulatory aftermath, analogy to traffic rules.

### Reusable Lessons

- For crisis stories, start with the accident, then rewind to the mechanism.
- Treat "bad behavior" as a product of incentives plus constraints.
- Close with institutional design, not moral judgment only.

## 6. 一口气了解伊朗经济

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uQJF5fcjFbg  
Corpus status: YouTube metadata indexed; zh subtitle checked locally for structural analysis only.

### One-Sentence Promise

The viewer will understand why Iran's economy is both fragile and resilient: oil, sanctions, political structure, subsidies, and informal networks create a distorted but persistent system.

### Hook Type

Country mystery. The opening contrasts war headlines with a deeper economic structure.

### Surface Question

How did Iran develop such a strange and durable economy under long-term pressure?

### Hidden Variable

Dual control system: resource rent and political-institutional control distribute economic power outside ordinary market channels.

### Mechanism Chain

Oil wealth attracts external control -> nationalization and intervention reshape politics -> revolutionary institutions consolidate power -> sanctions restrict normal trade and finance -> informal networks and subsidies keep the system running -> distortions accumulate into inflation, shortages, and energy stress.

### Section Map

1. Open with the gap between headlines and economic structure.
2. Build historical context from oil concessions and foreign intervention.
3. Explain political architecture and key power centers.
4. Show sanctions as a constraint that changes market behavior.
5. Map energy, subsidies, underground activity, and exports.
6. End with unresolved trajectory rather than a neat prediction.

### Evidence Pattern

Historical timeline, institutional actors, macro indicators, sanctions logic, sector examples.

### Reusable Lessons

- For country explainers, avoid pure chronology; make each period add one constraint.
- Name the hidden institutions or flows that ordinary GDP stories miss.
- End with scenario uncertainty when the future is genuinely open.
