# Xiao Lin Shuo Style Profile

Use this as a high-level style and craft profile, not as a license to imitate exact phrasing.

## Core Thesis

The style is not "say things casually." It is "make a complex system feel inevitable once the hidden variable is revealed." The surface is relaxed; the engine is disciplined.

## Durable Traits

- Question-first: begin from a puzzle the audience already half-recognizes.
- Mechanism-first: explain how incentives, constraints, feedback loops, metrics, or balance sheets move behavior.
- One-lens compression: choose one primary hidden variable and make other factors subordinate.
- Conversational density: use short spoken Chinese, but every paragraph should either clarify, surprise, or transition.
- Controlled analogy: explain abstract systems through concrete scenes, but return quickly to the mechanism.
- Micro-reversals: every 30-60 seconds, let the audience update their mental model.
- Evidence discipline: distinguish data, example, inference, and speculation.
- Reframing ending: the conclusion should make the opening question look too simple.

## Observed Episode-Level Patterns

Based on the public-safe analyses in `episode-breakdowns.md`, several patterns recur:

- Use a concrete recent trigger to justify the topic, then quickly move from news to mechanism.
- Put the most confusing contradiction before the definition, so definitions feel earned.
- Prefer "who is forced to do what because of which constraint" over "what is the concept."
- Use a tiny repeated example across multiple sections when explaining accounting or financial instruments.
- Treat institutions as incentive systems: regulator, bank, company, investor, trader, government, and consumer each get a constraint.
- End by upgrading the audience's question rather than only summarizing the answer.

## Voice Guidelines

Prefer:

- "你以为问题是 A，但真正卡住的是 B。"
- "我们先别急着站队，先把机制拆开。"
- "这个事儿听起来很玄，但其实可以压成一个变量。"
- "一旦这个变量变了，后面的行为就不奇怪了。"

Avoid:

- Long academic hedging before the audience understands the core mechanism.
- A list of unrelated causes.
- Dense names, dates, and numbers without immediate explanatory use.
- Jokes that only decorate the paragraph.
- Claiming to be Xiao Lin or using catchphrases as a substitute for structure.

## Rewrite Heuristics

1. Replace topic labels with causal questions.
2. Replace broad background with a contradiction or scene.
3. Replace multi-cause summaries with a dominant mechanism chain.
4. Convert abstract nouns into decisions made by a person, company, market, or institution.
5. Add "so what changes next?" after each major explanation.
6. Cut facts that do not change the audience's model.

## Common Failure Modes

- "像小 Lin" becomes only more colloquial. Fix by rebuilding the causal chain first.
- The draft has a good topic but no hidden variable. Fix by asking what single constraint changes everyone's behavior.
- The draft has many facts but no audience promise. Fix by stating what the viewer will understand after watching.
- The draft is clever but unverified. Fix by marking claims and adding a fact-check list.
